
## Olive Styling: Refined Neo‑Brutalism

### 1. Overall visual identity
- The board blends **neo‑brutalism** with **mid‑century craftsmanship** and a quiet, tactile elegance.
- Screens feel **grounded and inviting**: structural lines and bold geometry are softened by warm material cues, generous whitespace, and a sense of quiet confidence.
- Layouts remain **card‑driven**, but panels resemble curated desk objects: thick stock paper, leather‑bound folios, and polished wood organizers resting on a soft, textured canvas.

### 2. Color system
#### Palette character
- **Base backgrounds:** warm parchment, raw linen, soft oak, and cream. Pastel blocks shift to muted earth tones (dusty sage, warm terracotta, muted clay).
- **Primary brights:** heritage‑toned rather than neon: amber, oxblood/terracotta, deep aubergine, and muted teal. Used for headers, buttons, or structural bands.
- **Dark accents:** espresso, warm charcoal, and deep saddle brown for text and outlines. Pure black is avoided.
- **Retro terminal screens:** dark walnut or deep peat backgrounds with amber, warm green, or cream monospace text.

#### Usage rules
- One **dominant material color per screen** (e.g., a parchment canvas or oak band), with secondary elements in neutral or earth tones.
- Contrast remains high but feels **naturally lit**: warm darks on soft lights, or cream text on rich, dark surfaces.
- Gradients are rare and **material‑inspired** (e.g., soft wood grain transitions, linen‑to‑leather washes), always diffused and grain‑textured.

### 3. Layout and spacing
#### Page structure
- Single‑column mobile layout, centered with **generous, editorial padding**. Content breathes intentionally.
- Cards stack vertically with **consistent, measured gaps**. The rhythm feels deliberate, not cramped or overly playful.

#### Rhythm and density
- Each card follows **three zones**: a refined title/header strip, spacious main content, and a subtle footer for metadata or actions.
- Padding is comfortable but **proportionally balanced**. Elements align to a soft grid, with occasional intentional offsets that feel handcrafted rather than accidental.

### 4. Typography
#### Styling conventions
- Strong hierarchy maintained, but paired with a **refined type system**: a clean humanist or geometric sans for UI, complemented by a warm serif or elegant monospace for headlines and labels.
- Line spacing is open and breathable. Tracking is slightly increased for a more editorial feel.
- All‑caps are reserved for section dividers or primary actions, styled like **bespoke labels or archive stamps**.
- Hero headlines use minimal, thoughtful phrasing with ample whitespace, evoking a calm, magazine‑like tone.

### 5. Shape language and containers
#### Cards and panels
- Rounded corners are medium, borders are **2 px warm charcoal or espresso** with a subtle inner bevel or double‑line “stitch” effect.
- Cards feel like **thick stock or leather‑wrapped folios**. In earth‑toned screens, tall narrow cards list items in clean, evenly spaced rows.

#### Windows and frames
- Retro windows are reinterpreted as **desk organizers or archive cards**:
  - A solid header bar with a muted primary color.
  - Small, refined square/circle controls on the right, rendered with thin outlines.
  - Content area with clear inner padding and optional subtle grain overlay.

#### Dividers and grids
- Thin rules, dotted lines, or subtle dashed separators. Some views feature a **soft grid background** (warm grey or parchment lines) to evoke drafting paper or design boards.
- Library/list cards use even rectangular tiles with small, consistent gaps.

### 6. Iconography and illustration
#### Icons
- Simple, **refined line icons** (1.5 px weight) with consistent geometry. Slightly lighter than traditional brutalist chunkiness, but still bold enough to hold visual weight.
- Circular avatars and subtle face/emoji cues remain, but rendered with cleaner strokes and muted fills.

#### Illustrations
- Flat colors with **minimal shading**, occasionally layered with very faint paper or leather cross‑hatch textures.
- Decorative elements (small geometric marks, subtle dotted accents, archival stamps) surround cards, evoking **curated notebooks or studio moodboards**.

### 7. Buttons, controls, and states
Buttons retain a **tactile, physical quality** but feel like well‑made objects rather than toys.

#### Primary buttons
- Fill: heritage tones (amber, terracotta, aubergine, teal) with warm dark text, or neutral fills with refined colored borders.
- Borders: 1–2 px dark outlines with subtle corner rounding, evoking **cut stock or leather edging**.
- Size: tall, generous padding, short bold labels. Press states compress slightly with a warm, diffused shadow.

#### Secondary / ghost buttons
- Light or parchment backgrounds with dark borders, or underlined text in a warm serif.
- In retro windows, buttons resemble **classic OS dialog elements** but with softened bevels and muted edge highlights.

### 8. Surfaces, depth, and shadows
- Depth is guided by **structural outlines and warm, material‑aware shadows** rather than heavy blur.
- Where shadows appear:
  - Soft, warm‑toned, sitting directly beneath elements.
  - Occasionally stylized as a **1–2 px warm offset** to suggest layered paper or stacked leather.
- Cards rest on flat, textured canvases (parchment, subtle grid, or muted wood grain) that feel grounded and calm.

### 9. Micro‑interactions and animation cues
- Cards and panels enter with a **controlled spring**: quick, weighted, and smooth, like a well‑oiled drawer or leather flap closing.
- Hover/pressed states shift fill slightly, compress shadows, and add a subtle warm outline glow. No harsh inversions.
- Retro windows open or resize with **playful but precise easing**: slight overshoot, then settle into place, evoking craftsmanship over cartoonish motion.
